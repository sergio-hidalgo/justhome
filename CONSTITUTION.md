# Constitution

Binding for every spec. If a spec contradicts this file, this file wins and an issue is opened.

## 1. Product in one paragraph

A web app for buyers and sellers of resale homes in Spain. The user describes a property, their own position and priorities, and what they observe about the other side. A deterministic engine computes both sides' BATNA, the ZOPA, the Pareto frontier and a Nash-fair package, and tells the user what to offer, what to trade and when to walk away. Buyers: freemium + one-off packs. Sellers: account + subscription (first analysis free). Later, when buyer and seller of the same property are both on the platform, the app orchestrates one outcome that is good for both.

**Positioning.** Category: independent negotiation decision support for resale homes in Spain. Method: game theory, behavioural economics and sourced market evidence. Promise: better-informed buying and selling decisions, backed by transparent calculations and scenarios. It is **not** a property portal, an estate agency, a valuation (tasación) or financial, legal or mortgage advice, and it never promises the lowest purchase price or the highest sale price.

**Self-service only.** The user runs the analysis themselves. There is no personalised advisory, no human negotiator, and no brokerage or intermediation service.

**Three audiences.** The product serves (1) **buyers** and (2) **sellers**, who use the app, and (3) **AI assistants and agents**, which only **read**. Assistants answer a buyer's or seller's question and may cite our public knowledge and point the user to the app. They get **general information only**: the public knowledge layer (guides, market intelligence, methodology) as pages, Markdown copies, structured data and datasets. They never run an analysis for the user. The personalised analysis exists only inside the app, used by a person, under the tier rules (specs 20–22, decisions D-14…D-20).

## 2. Principles (non-negotiable)

1. **Decide, don't teach.** Every app screen (wizard, dashboard, modal) answers "what should I do now with this property?". Public knowledge pages (D-15) may explain, but each one answers one concrete decision question first, in the user's words, and ends in the matching tool entry.
2. **Estimates, never certainties.** Every counterpart figure carries a range (p10–p90) and a confidence level (low / medium / high). Copy says "estimado".
3. **Progressive profiling.** ≤ 3 questions per moment; taps over typing; "No lo sé / I don't know" is always a valid answer and keeps the prior.
4. **Non-manipulative.** Scripts are questions to learn facts. Never suggest exploiting private hardship. Never ask about the counterpart's debts, mortgage balance, health or family situation beyond a coarse "reason for hurry".
5. **Money first.** Housing issues are monetisable: show utility points and their euro equivalent side by side.
6. **The engine is the product.** It is pure, deterministic, versioned and tested before any UI depends on it.
7. **Functional UI first.** v1 UI is plain, accessible and complete (forms, lists, dashboards, analyses). Visual/marketing UX comes later and must not require engine or API changes.
8. **Privacy by design.** Counterparts are labels, not people. Joint mode never reveals one side's private inputs to the other. Machine-readable surfaces contain only public knowledge, never user data.
9. **Legible to people and machines.** Everything public is server-rendered, readable without JavaScript, states its sources, dates, units, geography and method, and has a machine-readable twin (JSON-LD that matches the visible content, a Markdown alternate, a CSV dataset where there is data). Engine outputs carry their assumptions and limitations as data, not only as prose.
10. **Decision support, not valuation, brokerage or advice.** Never call an output "tasación", "valor de mercado", "valor real" or "precio justo"; never promise a price outcome. Our figures are estimated negotiation ranges under stated assumptions.
11. **No back door.** No public or machine surface runs the engine on caller-supplied inputs. Every personalised analysis happens in the web app, with its tier limits (D-01…D-07). Automated use of the app is not allowed, and the app takes technical measures against it (D-17).

## 5. Engine invariants

- Pure functions, no I/O, no `Date.now()`, no `Math.random()`. Randomness comes from a seeded PRNG whose seed = hash of normalised inputs.
- Same inputs + same `ENGINE_VERSION` ⇒ byte-identical output.
- Utilities live on [0, 100]; weights sum to 1 (±1e-9).
- The Nash package, when one exists, is always inside the strict ZOPA and on the Pareto frontier.
- Market priors (margins, cost ranges, ITP rates) are **inputs**, never constants in code.

## 6. Conventions

- Money: integer euro cents in DB and API; euros (number) inside the engine; formatted with `Intl.NumberFormat(locale, {currency:'EUR'})` in UI.
- Dates: ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` strings; durations in days.
- IDs: UUID v4.
- Naming: DB `snake_case`, TS `camelCase`, routes Spanish slugs (English aliases redirect).
- Every API response with engine output includes `engineVersion`.
- Feature flags: `FLAG_JOINT_MODE`, `FLAG_EXPORT`.
- Public URLs (knowledge pages, studies, datasets, methodology terms) are stable: never renamed without a 301, never deleted (superseded content links to its successor).
- Canonical origin comes from `PUBLIC_SITE_URL`; every public page has a canonical URL and `hreflang` es/en/x-default.
- Machine-readable copies (Markdown alternates, datasets, JSON-LD) contain resolved text and raw values, never bare i18n keys.

## 7. Glossary

| Term                                   | Meaning                                                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Issue                                  | A negotiable dimension (price, closing date…)                                                                                                     |
| Package (ω)                            | One level chosen for every issue                                                                                                                  |
| Value function u                       | Maps an issue level to [0,100] for one side                                                                                                       |
| Weight w                               | Importance of an issue for one side; Σw = 1                                                                                                       |
| U(ω)                                   | Σ w·u — total utility of a package for one side                                                                                                   |
| BATNA b                                | Utility of the best alternative to agreement                                                                                                      |
| Surplus s                              | U(ω) − b                                                                                                                                          |
| Strict ZOPA                            | Packages with U > b for both sides                                                                                                                |
| Pareto frontier                        | Non-dominated packages in the ZOPA                                                                                                                |
| Nash package                           | argmax s_me · s_other on the frontier                                                                                                             |
| KS point                               | Frontier point equalising proportional gains toward each side's ideal                                                                             |
| Scenario A/B/C                         | Seller lives there & hasn't bought / already bought (double burden) / needs to sell to buy                                                        |
| Signal                                 | An observation logged by the user that updates the counterpart estimate                                                                           |
| Slot                                   | Right to create one buyer property from a pack                                                                                                    |
| Reservation price                      | The worst terms a side would still accept (buyer: max price; seller: min price), derived from its BATNA                                           |
| Nash package vs Nash equilibrium       | We use the Nash **bargaining** solution (max product of surpluses). It is not the Nash equilibrium of a strategic game; copy never conflates them |
| Pareto frontier vs Pareto distribution | Pareto **efficiency** (no package better for one side without being worse for the other). The Pareto _distribution_ is unrelated and unused       |
| Asking-to-sale discount                | (asking − final price) / asking for the **same** property (market input, `margin_kind = asking_to_sale`)                                          |
| Market price gap                       | Gap between average asking prices and average transaction prices, usually **different** properties (`market_gap`)                                 |
| Listing price cut                      | Reduction of the asking price while listed, without a closing price (`listing_cut`)                                                               |
| Estimated negotiation range            | Engine output: the counterpart's reservation p10–p90 for **one** property under stated assumptions; never a market statistic                      |
| Knowledge page                         | Public guide, market or methodology page (D-15)                                                                                                   |
| Decision-intent cluster                | Guides grouped by the decision the user faces at a stage (e.g. "received a low offer"), not by keyword                                            |
| Agent                                  | An AI assistant or automated client that reads our public pages for a user; it never uses the app                                                 |
| Deep link                              | Public link to `/comprador#nueva` or `/vendedor#nueva` with property facts as query parameters; it prefills the wizard and nothing else           |
| GEO                                    | Generative-engine optimisation: making the site discoverable, understandable and citable by AI assistants                                         |

## 9. Known corrections to the source notes

The DeepSeek worked examples contain errors; fixtures in `fixtures/` are recomputed and are the reference:

- Housing example: seller BATNA was given as 56.25, but with the stated seller value table (30 days = 0 for the seller) it is **26.25**. The ZOPA, frontier and Nash package change accordingly.
- Housing ZOPA table utilities did not match the stated weights.
- Freelance logrolling paragraph contradicted itself; the freelance metrics (ZOPA 5, frontier 3, Nash = 10,000 € / 4 weeks / 60%) are correct.
- Figures quoted in the GEO strategy notes (issue 005, e.g. 6.2 % average negotiation discount, 15 % asking-vs-transaction gap across 57 cities, notarial-data portal) are **leads, not data**: they enter only through the market CSV with a verified source, `as_of` and `margin_kind` (see `docs/market-sources.md`).
- Market figures in the notes are superseded by sourced 2026 data in `data/market/2026-10-12.csv` (see `docs/market-sources.md`): e.g. Granada's negotiation gap is 9 % (Fotocasa H1 2026), not 35 %; no Madrid figure is published, so the national proxy of 6 % (idealista Q2 2026 average listing cut) applies with low confidence.
